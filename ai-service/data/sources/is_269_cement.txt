Document: Ordinary Portland Cement — Specification (demo reference: IS 269 : 2015)
Page: 7
Clause: 4.1 — Grades and Composition

Summary of scope:
This demo document illustrates how an ordinary Portland cement standard
typically classifies cement by strength grade and sets composition limits.

Illustrative parameters covered:
- Grade classification: cement is commonly grouped into strength grades
  (illustrated here as 33, 43 and 53), named after the minimum compressive
  strength, in MPa, the cement must reach after 28 days of curing.
- Composition limits: maximum permissible proportions of magnesium oxide,
  sulphur trioxide, insoluble residue and loss on ignition, to keep the
  cement chemically stable over time.
- Fineness requirement: a minimum specific surface area so the cement
  hydrates and gains strength at an expected rate.
- Setting time: minimum initial setting time and maximum final setting
  time, so the cement is workable long enough but sets reliably.

Illustrative clause 4.1 (Grades and Composition):
A batch of cement can only be labelled with a given grade if it meets both
the compressive-strength requirement for that grade at the specified curing
age and the composition limits above; failing either disqualifies the
grade claim.

Who this is relevant to:
Cement manufacturers, builders and contractors, structural engineers
specifying materials, and hardware retailers stocking certified cement.
