Document: Footwear — General Requirements and Test Methods (demo reference: IS 15298 (Part 2) : 2016)
Page: 3
Clause: 3.4 — Sole Bonding Strength

Summary of scope:
This demo document illustrates the kind of quality and durability tests a
general footwear standard typically applies to soles and upper materials.

Illustrative parameters covered:
- Sole bonding strength: the force needed to peel the sole away from the
  upper must exceed a defined minimum, so the sole doesn't detach with
  normal wear.
- Upper material durability: flex-cracking resistance so the upper doesn't
  crack after a specified number of repeated bending cycles.
- Abrasion resistance: material loss after a standard abrasion test must
  stay below a defined limit, indicating the sole will wear gradually
  rather than failing quickly.
- Slip resistance (where applicable): minimum coefficient of friction on
  specified test surfaces for footwear marketed for wet or oily
  environments.

Illustrative clause 3.4 (Sole Bonding Strength):
A sample fails this clause if the average peel force across test
specimens falls below the minimum threshold, even if individual specimens
pass — the standard evaluates the batch average, not just the best result.

Who this is relevant to:
Footwear manufacturers, quality inspectors performing batch testing, and
retailers stocking BIS-compliant footwear.
