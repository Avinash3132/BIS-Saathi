Document: Drinking Water — Specification (demo reference: IS 10500 : 2012)
Page: 14
Clause: 5.2 — Permissible Limits

Summary of scope:
This demo document illustrates the kind of requirements a drinking water
standard typically specifies so packaged and municipal water can be
considered fit for human consumption.

Illustrative parameters covered:
- Physical parameters: turbidity, colour, odour and taste, within limits
  that keep water visually and sensorially acceptable.
- Chemical parameters: acceptable ranges for pH, total dissolved solids,
  hardness, chloride, fluoride, and limits on selected heavy metals.
- Biological parameters: absence of E. coli and other coliform organisms
  in a defined sample volume, to rule out faecal contamination.
- Testing cadence: periodic sampling and laboratory testing intervals for
  bottlers and municipal suppliers to demonstrate ongoing compliance.

Illustrative clause 5.2 (Permissible Limits):
Where the acceptable limit for a parameter is not met, water may still be
supplied only if it stays within a wider "permissible limit in the absence
of an alternate source", and only for parameters where the standard allows
this relaxation; several parameters (in particular biological ones) have
no such relaxation and must always meet the acceptable limit.

Who this is relevant to:
Consumers, packaged drinking water bottlers, water purifier manufacturers,
and municipal water supply departments preparing for BIS certification or
routine compliance checks.
